## T3-R1: the packet's time and the last trade's time are two fields

The decoder (`terminal/src/angel/decode.rs`) now reads three: the packet's exchange timestamp (bytes 35–43, milliseconds), the **last-traded timestamp** (123–131) and the **sequence number** (27–35), as the broker's official decoder documents them. The last-traded field states no unit; it is read by magnitude (an epoch time of 2000 to 2099 in seconds or in milliseconds; the ranges do not overlap; anything else, zero included, is none). That agrees with how `src/athena/live/angel.py` already reads the field (seconds) and was **not** checked against a live stream.

| Wire field | Page field | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `t` | `packetTime` | the exchange's timestamp of the **packet** that gave the quote its content. The value is what `t` always held; only its label was wrong, so an older page is not broken by it. A new packet need not carry a new trade |
| `lt` (new) | `time` | the exchange's time of the **last trade**, from the packet's own last-traded field. An absent field (zero) keeps the held time when the last price is unchanged (the last trade did not vanish) and is **unknown** otherwise; it is never the packet's time. A server that predates `lt` gives no trade time, and the page does not fall back to `t` |
| `tf` (new, only when non-empty) | `timeFaults` | a time the server **refused** (not an epoch time, or ahead of its clock beyond 60 s), with the field, the value as received and the reason |
| `dly` | `deliveryDelayMs` | now receipt less the **packet's** time, for a packet newer than the greatest seen; null on first sight |
| `r`, `bs`, `ooo` | as before | unchanged; `bs` is still a receipt time and is labelled so |

Book freshness stays receipt-based and is not replaced by a last-trade-age test. Tests: the decoder (own offsets, both units, malformed values, neighbouring fields undisturbed); the store (the packet time moves without the trade time on a book-only update, the trade time moves on a trade, an unavailable trade time, refused values); the route with real packet bytes; and a **fixture generated by the Rust code** (`terminal/web/test/fixtures/deriv-quotes.cases.json`: decoder, store, route rows) that the page tests read (`optionsession.test.ts`).

## T3-R2: ordering survives a packet with no time

The store keeps an **ordering watermark** per contract (the greatest valid packet time applied) apart from the quote it last produced, and the last applied sequence number. A packet is **older** when its time is below the watermark, or equal to it with a lower sequence, or has no time and a lower sequence. An older packet is discarded, counted (`ooo`) and moves no time and not the watermark. A packet with no usable time (absent or refused) is applied in arrival order and never touches the watermark, so *known, missing, older* discards the older packet and *known, missing, newer* applies it. A repeated identical packet is no change. `DerivQuotes::reset_sequences` forgets the sequences (not the watermark), and the stream calls it on **every new connection**; this is tested with a stand-in broker that restarts its numbering on the second connection.

## T3-R3: one session policy, in the server's clock

`terminal/web/src/options/session.ts`, policy `option_session` version 1. The feed's `session()` and `badge()` are one decision.

| Evidence (server clock) | State, reason |
|---|---|
| some contract has a receipt within 120 s of the server's now **and** a packet time within 120 s of that receipt | `live`, `recent_packets_with_current_event_times` |
| receipts are recent but every packet time is old (a replay, a closed market's snapshot, an exchange clock far from the server's) | `last_session`, `event_times_old` |
| receipts are recent but no packet carries a usable time | `unknown`, `no_event_time` (quotes are labelled `session_unverified`) |
| no receipt within 120 s (a feed gone quiet, or cached contracts held by the server) | `last_session`, `no_recent_packets` (the page cannot tell a shut market from a stopped feed: it has no calendar, and says so) |
| no receipt at all, or only ones ahead of the server's clock beyond 5 s (a server that sends none, a clock that rolled back) | `unknown`, `no_receipts` |

An exchange time that merely **advances** is no longer evidence (a replay advances too). An observation is the newest packet **per contract**, so a repeated identical packet (the same receipt), rows re-delivered after a reconnect, or a switch to another expiry's cached contracts (old receipts) are not activity. A fresh book with an old last trade is live (the session reads the packet's time, not the trade's). A browser clock ahead of or behind the server's changes nothing: ages and the session are in the server's clock. The decision carries the `reason`, the `evidence` (server now, newest receipt, newest packet time, the lag between them, receipt age, contracts seen, how many were current) and the policy id and version. The badge reads `live prices`, `last session prices` (the title says which reason) or `session unknown`.

## T3-R4: the forward keeps its inputs' labels, and its inputs must be in step

`decideForward` judges each input quote (the future, or the call and put of each parity strike used) by the same rules as an option. A forward now carries:

- `inputs`: role, strike, the input's own state, flags and book time;
- `inputFlags`: every label of every input, and `forward_input_labelled` (a label), so a wide, delayed or out-of-order future still labels the forward, the chain's badge and every option's volatility;
- `spanMs` and **`forward_unsynced` across the inputs**: their book times must lie within 5 minutes of each other. A call and a put 10 minutes apart, each under the 15-minute limit, are no longer a fit forward, and neither are strikes read at different times;
- `forward_time_invalid` and `forward_input_withheld`;
- the record of what was tried: `candidates` (each source evaluated, in order, used / rejected / absent, with the reason `no_future`, `no_quote`, `withheld` with its flags, `no_usable_pair`, `far_from_spot`, `grown_from_spot`), `excluded` (contracts that would have been parity inputs, with a quote and a price on both sides of the strike, but failed their own rules) and the unchanged `rejected`.

## T3-R5: required inputs are always listed

`quality.required` is always the forward, the at-the-money call and the at-the-money put, each with a `presence`:

- `present`: judged as usual;
- `absent`: the chain has an at-the-money strike but no contract on that side. `atm_side_absent`, **label**; the headline volatility is read from the other side and `coverage` is `call_only` or `put_only`;
- `no_atm`: no strikes, no forward to find the strike from, or a strike with neither contract. `no_atm`, **withhold**, `coverage: none`.

A contract the feed has not spoken about is now `no_quote` (**withhold**), no longer the generic "no two-sided book". The tooltip names the missing side and the coverage.

## T3-R6: a future or invalid time is never fresh

Validity is decided apart from age. A time that is not a number, not an epoch time (before 2000), or ahead of the decision's clock beyond the declared tolerance is **invalid**, recorded in `inputs.timeFaults` with the field, the value as received (a non-number as its text), the reason (`not_finite`, `implausible`, `future`, with how far ahead) and the origin (this page, or the server's refusal).

| Clock | Tolerance (`ahead <= limit` is valid) |
|---|---|
| server clock (a receipt: `bookSeenAt`, `receivedAt`) | 5 s |
| exchange clock (a trade time, a book time a source gives, the packet time) | 60 s |

Within the tolerance the age is nil, never negative. Consequences: `invalid_book_time` **withhold** (a source that gives a book time and gives it wrongly is not trusted for the other), `invalid_trade_time` **label** (the book is still read), `forward_time_invalid` **withhold**. Ages are clamped at zero, and the chain's "newest quote" age ignores times that are not valid, so no chain can report ordinary live or fit status through a negative age (tested with every time an hour ahead, as after a server clock that rolled back).

## Policy version 2

`POLICY_V2` replaces `POLICY_V1`; the earlier rules are unchanged. New limits `maxReceiptAheadMs` 5 s and `maxExchangeAheadMs` 60 s. New flags, each with a declared consequence: `no_quote`, `invalid_book_time`, `invalid_trade_time`, `session_unverified`, `forward_time_invalid`, `forward_input_labelled`, `forward_input_withheld`, `atm_side_absent`, `no_atm`. `forward_unsynced` also applies between the inputs of a forward. Every decision records version 2; its inputs gain the packet time, the valid book time used, how far ahead it was, and every invalid time.

## Compatibility effects

- **Wire:** `/api/derivs/quotes` rows and the socket's derivative frames gain `lt` and, only when a time was refused, `tf`; `t` keeps its value (the label changes); nothing is removed. A page older than this change reads `t` as it always did; a page newer than the server reads no `lt` and treats the trade time as unknown.
- **Rust:** `Tick` gains `last_trade_raw`, `last_trade_ms`, `sequence`; `DerivQuote` gains `last_trade_ms`, `time_faults`, `sequence` (not serialised); `DerivQuotes::reset_sequences`; `MAX_EXCHANGE_AHEAD_MS` (60 s) is the store's tolerance and equals the page policy's `maxExchangeAheadMs` (kept equal by hand: a Rust test pins the constant at 60,000, a web test pins the policy; no test crosses the language boundary).
- **Page:** `POLICY_V1` is replaced by `POLICY_V2` (decisions made under version 1 are not reproduced by it); `SessionState` gains `reason`, `evidence`, `policy`; `LiveFeed.badge()` is required; `Quote` gains `packetTime` and `timeFaults`; `ForwardDecision`, `ChainQuality` and `QuoteInputs` gain the fields above; `parityForwardDetail` also returns each input's role and strike.
- **Intentional behaviour changes:**
  1. an exchange time that advances no longer makes the feed live; an exchange clock hours from the server's reads as `last session prices`;
  2. a server that sends no receipt times gives `session unknown` (it used to read live or last session from `t`);
  3. a parity forward from inputs 5 to 15 minutes apart is withheld (it was accepted);
  4. a chain with no contract on one side of the money is labelled, not `ok`;
  5. a quote the feed has not spoken about is `no_quote`;
  6. a future-dated time withholds.
- **Existing tests changed on purpose**, each marked in its file: `optionquality.test.ts` (policy version 2, forward inputs now 2 and 5 minutes apart, `no_quote`, the input key list) and `optionslive.test.ts` (the row mapping uses `lt` for the trade time; "live when exchange times keep moving" now asserts the opposite; the old session test reads receipts and packet times; the stand-in sends `lt` and a packet time near its receipt; "no book time" now reads `session unknown`).
- **Not changed:** `black76.ts`, the e2e spec (`options.spec.ts`; not run), Python.

### T3-C1: invalid receipt and packet times, and server refusals, now have declared consequences

**Defect (reproduced by the review's four probes):** `checkTime` found an invalid `receivedAt` or `packetTime`, and the server's `tf` refusals were kept, but only book and trade faults changed a decision. A contract with a receipt or packet time an hour ahead, or one whose packet time the server refused, was `ok` with no flag; an unknown session was labelled only for the reason `no_event_time`. Another contract's valid packets could make the aggregate session live and so hide the fault.

**Policy `option_quote_quality` version 3** (`POLICY_V3`, replacing `POLICY_V2`; limits unchanged):

| Time | Invalid when | Flag | Consequence | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| book time (`bookTime`, `bookSeenAt`) | not a number, before 2000, or ahead beyond 5 s (receipt) / 60 s (exchange) | `invalid_book_time` | **withhold** (unchanged) | the age of the price is unknowable |
| last trade time | same, or refused by the server (`lt`) | `invalid_trade_time` | label (unchanged) | the book is still read from its own valid time |
| **receipt** (`receivedAt`) | same, 5 s tolerance | `invalid_receipt_time` | **label** (new) | the book's age comes from its own time; the price is kept but the contract is not called fit |
| **packet time** (`packetTime`) | same, 60 s tolerance, **or refused by the server (`t`), or any server refusal of a field the page does not know** | `invalid_packet_time` | **label** (new) | same; an unknown field is never silent |
| session | `unknown` for **any** reason (`no_event_time`, `no_receipts`, or no session assessed at all) | `session_unverified` | **label** (widened from `no_event_time` only) | an unknown session is not verified, whatever the reason |

The consequence is applied **per contract, in `assessQuote`, before the forward and the chain are read**, from that contract's own times, so another valid contract that makes the aggregate session live does not remove it. The labels travel: a forward whose input is labelled carries `forward_input_labelled` and the input's labels in `inputFlags` (all options read against it are labelled); an at-the-money call or put that is labelled makes the chain `labelled` and the badge says `quotes labelled` with the reason in its title; a wing that is labelled is not a required input, so the badge stays `live prices`, but the side is labelled, its cell is marked and titled, and `rows.labelled` counts it (the chain's tooltip says "1 labelled"). The forward itself is also labelled `session_unverified` when the session is unknown. `inputs.receivedAt` and `inputs.packetTime` hold valid values only. Labelling, not withholding, is the declared consequence for the two new flags; withholding the price because a packet's own timestamp is wrong would discard a book whose age is known from its own valid time. That is a policy choice, recorded here and pinned by tests (a mutant that makes either flag withhold is caught).

**Behaviour change to know:** any assembly with no session information (`session` absent, as in the synthetic feed) is now `labelled` with `session_unverified`; no existing test asserted otherwise. Old-trade-beside-fresh-book is unchanged (no flag).

**Tests** (`terminal/web/test/optioninvalidtimes.test.ts`, new, 24): the review's four probes; the 5 s and 60 s limits at the edge; not-a-number, implausible and absent times; unknown server field; invalid book plus invalid receipt together; the old-trade rule; mixed chains in which a valid contract makes the session live (ATM call packet time ahead, ATM put refused by the server, ATM call receipt ahead, the future's receipt ahead, a parity forward input, a wing, an unknown session) asserting the row flags, the forward's labels, the required-input list, the chain status and `worst`, and the summary text; and the same through the live feed and the page, asserting the badge text, `data-kind`, the badge title, the cell's class and title. ### T3-C2: the mutation classifier no longer counts suite-load errors as caught

**Defect (reproduced, red evidence `runs/20261116-135837-harness-v1-probe-red/`):** `mutate.py` (kept as `mutate.v1.py`) took any `FAIL file` line as a failing test, ignored its build-error check whenever such a line existed, and called every other non-zero exit caught. The review's probe (`FAIL test/synthetic.test.ts`, `SyntaxError`, `Tests no tests`, exit 1) came out `caught`.

**`mutate.py` version 2** judges each execution's **machine-readable result**: vitest's JSON report (`--reporter=json --outputFile.json=` is added to each command), or, for Rust, libtest's stable text output parsed into the same normalised report (libtest's JSON format needs a nightly compiler). Outcomes: `caught` needs at least one **assertion failure** (`AssertionError`, or a Rust `assert*` panic) in a test that **passed on the unmutated tree**, no suite that failed to load, and the baseline's number of tests; `SURVIVED` needs all tests passing, exit 0 and the baseline's count; **`mixed`** (assertions beside suites that did not load or a different test count) and **`error_only`** (tests failed by an exception, none by an assertion) and `unattributed` (an assertion failed outside the tests the mutation names in `expect`) are separate classes, never "caught"; `invalid` covers build, transform, import and setup errors, suites that did not load, no tests, a missing or unreadable report, an inconsistent report, a non-zero exit with no failing test, a failing test the baseline never ran, and test-count drift; `timeout` kills the process tree; `harness_error` keeps the log. Logs are written as **raw bytes before anything reads them**. The baseline (unmutated tree: exit 0, tests reported, none failing, no suite failing to load) is run for the default command **and once for each mutation's own `command` override**; a default baseline that is not clean stops the run as INVALID, an unclean override baseline makes that mutation `invalid`. The run exits 0 only if every mutation is `caught`.

**Tests of the harness** (`scripts/test_mutate.py`, 16): a synthetic runner and an inert target file cover the review's acceptance list (passing baseline; syntax, import and setup errors; no tests; missing and unreadable report; unexplained exit; timeout; a real assertion failure; a surviving mutant; a mixed run; a failing test the baseline never ran; count drift; an inconsistent report; undecodable output kept in the log; an override command's own baseline; a pattern error; a harness error), plus the text parsers. A real-vitest self-test (five mutants on `quality.ts`) classified: syntax error → `invalid`, unresolvable import → `invalid`, comment-only change → `SURVIVED`, throwing code → `error_only` (54 tests failed, none by an assertion: it would have been "caught" before), changed limit → `caught`.

### Limits that remain

Everything under "Not established, and limits" below still holds, and in addition: the two new consequences (label, not withhold) are a policy choice, not calibrated on real data; the Rust mutation report is parsed from libtest's text, not JSON, and a Rust test meant to kill a mutant must fail by `assert_eq!`/`assert_ne!` or a macro whose message says "assertion" (a plain `assert!(c, "msg")` or `.expect` reads as `error_only`); the web page checks are jsdom; the e2e suite was not run; the harness cannot say a mutant is *semantically* meaningful, only that an assertion in a baseline-passing test failed with it.

## Not established, and limits

- The last-traded field's unit is read by magnitude and was not checked on a live stream. The sequence number's behaviour (per connection, per token, monotone) is assumed from the broker's decoder; **no live stream was seen**. The sequence orders packets only at an equal packet time or when a packet has no time, which bounds the exposure if the assumption is wrong.
- A packet time up to 60 s ahead is accepted and becomes the watermark, so honest packets stamped in the following minute are discarded as older (counted in `ooo`, visible as a label). The tolerance is a policy choice, not calibrated on real data.
- The page cannot tell a shut market from a stopped feed (no calendar): both read `last session prices`, labelled an observation. An exchange clock more than 2 minutes behind the server's cannot be told from a replay.
- The thresholds (15 min, 5 min, 120 s, 25%, 5 s, 60 s) are **not calibrated on real market data**, and a quiet but valid standing book is still reported stale after 15 minutes.
- The page was exercised in jsdom with rows made by the Rust code, not with a real socket or browser. The Playwright e2e suite was not run. One machine, one run of each suite. The guard is a tripwire and the Rust stage enforces nothing at run time.
